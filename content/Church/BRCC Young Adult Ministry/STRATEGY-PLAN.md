# 14 Young Adult Ministry Strategy — Comprehensive Final Document

## Context

We have 13 research documents covering demographics, national trends, church case studies, ministry models, best practices, strategic analysis, competitive landscape, market share, and BRCC's own internal assessment (including 6 survey responses). We also have two CRITICAL docs (Analysis + Recommendations) that served as scaffolding during the research process.

The goal is to create a comprehensive, stand-alone strategy document that:
- Synthesizes all research into a single narrative readable without the other docs
- Makes the case for the opportunity (leadership audience)
- Presents 2-3 ministry model options with a recommendation
- References survey/internal data sparingly — no names, anonymized, framed as directional confirmation of research
- Supersedes CRITICAL-ANALYSIS and CRITICAL-RECOMMENDATIONS (absorbs their honest critiques)
- Informs and inspires — seeks alignment on approach, then lets next steps emerge from discussion
- Will later serve as the basis for a presentation
- Markdown first, written with clean structure for later conversion to a formatted document

## Target Length
~3,500-5,000 words (~10-14 formatted pages). Comprehensive but scannable: bold key findings, use tables where appropriate, tight prose.

## Survey Data Guidelines
- **No names** — fully anonymized ("one respondent noted...", "survey responses suggest...")
- **Used sparingly** — early directional signal, not evidence. Always paired with external research.
- **Exception**: Competitive landscape data (which churches peers attend) is useful intel even at n=6 and corroborates Doc 11
- **Always qualified**: "early survey responses (n=6)" or similar

- What young adults want: community, authenticity, spiritual depth, mentorship, belonging (from Docs 05, 04)

**Part 2: The Competitive Landscape** (~500-700 words)
- Central Indiana church landscape: who's serving young adults and how (from Doc 11)
- Where BRCC-area young adults' peers go and why — College Park (Fireside), Traders Point (The Porch), Northview, Real Life, Harvest (from survey + Doc 11)
- Market share reality: ~900 realistically addressable young adults, growing over time (from Doc 12)
- BRCC's competitive position: what makes BRCC different (expository teaching, genuine community, intergenerational culture, existing informal networks, facilities)
- Key insight: competition is regional churches with established YA ministries, not local suburban churches

**Part 3: BRCC Today — What We're Building On** (~500-700 words)
- Current young adult engagement: athletics, men's study, informal networks (from Doc 13)
- Organizational readiness: budget flexibility, facilities, leadership support (from Doc 13)
- Cultural fit: expository preaching, casual atmosphere, willingness to tackle hard topics
- Honest gaps: no current staff capacity, digital presence weak, historical context unknown, communication channels not reaching young adults

**Part 4: What Works — Lessons from Research and Successful Churches** (~600-800 words)
- Common elements of thriving young adult ministries (from Docs 06, 07, 08):
  - Dedicated champion/leader (THE non-negotiable)
  - Small groups as foundation
  - Mix of spiritual, social, service programming
  - Young adult empowerment (ministry WITH not TO)
  - Church-wide culture, not siloed program
- The Growing Young framework (Doc 04): culture transformation alongside programming — addresses the "organic vs. formal" tension by showing intentional culture cultivation is needed even with organic growth
  - Reference the 6 commitments briefly as a lens, not as the organizing framework
- The integrated model: serving both singles and young marrieds in suburban context (from Docs 09, 10)
- Common pitfalls to avoid: under-resourcing, creating silos, copying without contextualizing, evaluating too early

**Part 5: Recommended Strategy — Three Options** (~700-900 words)

Present 3 approaches, each with description, pros/cons, approximate investment, and fit for BRCC:

**Option A: Grassroots — Small Groups First**
- Launch 2-3 small groups (gender-specific + co-ed) building on existing networks
- Monthly social gathering to create broader community
- Volunteer-led; existing staff sponsor
- Add programming based on response
- *Pros*: Low cost, low risk, organic growth
- *Cons*: Research consistently shows ministries without a dedicated champion tend to stall; depends on volunteer consistency; slow to critical mass
- *Investment*: Minimal ($2-5K/year for materials and events)

**Option B: Full Launch — Hire and Build**
- Hire a young adult pastor/director
- Launch with weekly gathering (worship + teaching + small groups) plus social programming
- Modeled after successful programs (College Park Fireside, etc.) scaled for BRCC
- *Pros*: Fastest to critical mass, dedicated leadership, signals institutional priority
- *Cons*: Highest upfront cost, hire-dependent timeline, risk if wrong hire
- *Investment*: $50-80K/year (staff + programming)

**Option C: Dedicated Leader + Phased Programming (Recommended)**
- Hire a part-time or full-time young adult leader as first step
- Phase 1 (months 1-6): Small groups + monthly gathering + build on athletics community
- Phase 2 (months 6-12): Scale to bi-weekly or weekly gatherings based on response
- Phase 3 (year 2+): Full programming mix — worship nights, service projects, social events
- *Pros*: Addresses "champion" need immediately, builds organically, adapts to response, balances the organic instinct with intentional leadership
- *Cons*: Moderate investment, still depends on right hire
- *Investment*: $30-60K/year initially, scaling with growth

**Rationale for recommending Option C**: Every source — external research, case studies, and early internal feedback — converges on two things: (1) you need a dedicated champion, and (2) small groups are the foundation. Option C addresses both from day one while allowing programming to scale based on actual response rather than assumptions. It also respects the instinct toward organic growth while providing the intentional leadership that research shows is essential.

**Honest Risks and Open Questions**
- No one on current staff has capacity to lead this — a hire is required for Options B and C
- Senior pastor is supportive but not yet actively championing; Growing Young research shows senior leadership buy-in is a critical success factor
- We don't know why young adults have left BRCC in the past — historical patterns could reveal barriers
- The "organic vs. formal" tension is real: formal programming without culture change fails, but organic connection without intentional leadership tends to plateau
- BRCC's digital presence is weak — young adults discover and evaluate churches online before visiting
- Small survey sample (n=6) means our understanding of BRCC young adults' needs is still preliminary

**Part 6: Measuring Success** (~300-400 words)
- Quantitative: realistic attendance projections from Doc 12 (20-40 Year 1, 40-80 Year 2, 50-120 Year 3)
- Qualitative: Are young adults leading, inviting friends, serving church-wide, growing spiritually? (draws on Growing Young and other research)
- Timeline expectations: 3-5 year horizon before major evaluation
- Year 1 is about foundation, not numbers
- Warning signs: declining volunteer engagement, leader burnout, young adults not inviting peers

**Part 7: Next Steps and Discussion Questions** (~300-400 words)
- Decisions for leadership to make together
  - Which option (or hybrid) fits BRCC's vision and capacity?
  - What's the timeline for a hiring decision?
  - Who champions this at the leadership level?
- Key questions the research can't answer (requires BRCC-specific discernment)
  - What's BRCC's unique value proposition for young adults vs. regional competitors?
  - How does this integrate with the church's broader vision and priorities?
  - What does success look like in BRCC's own terms?
- Immediate action items regardless of which option is chosen
  - Expand the survey / conduct listening sessions with more young adults
  - Identify a staff sponsor or interim champion
  - Begin the conversation about hiring timeline and budget
  - Consider taking the Growing Young Assessment (fulleryouthinstitute.org/growingyoung) to baseline BRCC's current state against the 6 commitments
- The demographic window is open now — Hancock County growth means the addressable population is increasing, and early investment positions BRCC ahead of the curve
