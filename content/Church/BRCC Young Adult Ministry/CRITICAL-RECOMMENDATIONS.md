# Areas for Further Investigation
**Purpose**: Identify next steps to address gaps in young adult ministry research

---

## Executive Summary

The comprehensive young adult ministry research demonstrates strong external analysis but reveals critical gaps requiring further investigation. This document outlines prioritized areas for additional research and assessment to support implementation planning.

**Target Ministry**: Ages 22-32, integrated approach serving both singles and young married couples

**Key Finding**: External landscape is well-researched. Internal BRCC assessment and competitive analysis are the primary missing components.

## TIER 1: Essential Internal Assessment

These investigations are foundational for moving from external research to implementation planning.

### 1. Current Young Adult Presence at BRCC

**What We Need to Know:**
- How many current attenders are ages 22-32?
- Where do they live geographically?
- What's their engagement level beyond Sunday services?
- Do they feel served by current programming?
- Are informal young adult connections already happening?
- Why do they choose BRCC?

**Investigation Methods:**
- Church database analysis for age distribution
- Sunday service observation and informal counting
- Interviews with 5-10 current young adults (if any)
- Children's ministry data (parents in 25-35 age range)
- Staff observations and insights

**Questions for Young Adult Interviews:**
1. Why do you attend BRCC?
2. What do you value most about BRCC?
3. What do you wish BRCC offered for people your age?
4. Would you participate in young adult programming if offered?
5. What would make you invite friends?
6. What churches do your peers attend? Why?

**Expected Outcome:**
- Baseline attendance count (even if rough estimate)
- Understanding of current young adult experience
- Identification of existing informal connections
- Insights into BRCC's appeal (or lack thereof) to demographic

**Why This Matters:**
Can't build strategy without knowing current state. May discover opportunities already present or obstacles not anticipated.

### 2. Budget Reality Assessment

**What We Need to Know:**
- BRCC's total annual budget
- Current allocation percentages to various ministries
- Budget approval process and timeline
- Realistic funding available for new ministry
- What would need to be reallocated
- Multi-year commitment capacity

**Investigation Methods:**
- Review with finance leadership or treasurer
- Examine recent budget meetings and allocations
- Compare to similar church ministry investments
- Assess BRCC's financial health and flexibility

**Specific Budget Questions:**
1. What's BRCC's total operating budget?
2. What percentage goes to children's, youth, other age ministries?
3. Is there capacity for $60K-90K new ministry investment?
4. Would this require reallocation or is new funding possible?
5. What's the budget approval process?
6. Can multi-year (3-5 year) commitment be made?
7. What contingencies exist if financial situation changes?

**Expected Outcome:**
- Clear understanding of available resources
- Realistic budget expectations ($9K vs. $60K-90K)
- Knowledge of approval process and timeline
- Identification of funding sources or constraints

**Why This Matters:**
Investment required ($60K-90K annually) is significantly different from research suggestion ($9K). Need to know if this is financially viable for BRCC.

### 3. Staffing Options and Process

**What We Need to Know:**
- Can existing staff add this responsibility?
- What's the hiring process and typical timeline?
- What salary range is standard for BRCC staff?
- Who would supervise young adult pastor?
- What's the staff structure and reporting relationships?

**Investigation Methods:**
- Discussion with senior pastor about staff capacity
- Review hiring procedures with personnel leadership
- Assess current staff workload and bandwidth
- Examine similar hiring timelines at BRCC
- Compare staff salaries to determine appropriate range

**Specific Staffing Questions:**
1. Is any current staff available to lead this (even temporarily)?
2. If hiring, what's the realistic timeline? (3 months? 6 months?)
3. What salary range is appropriate? ($40K-50K? $50K-70K?)
4. Who supervises young adult pastor? (Senior pastor? Elder oversight?)
5. What authority does this role have? (Budget? Programming decisions?)
6. How are staff performance and success evaluated?

**Expected Outcome:**
- Clear staffing plan (hire vs. assign existing staff)
- Realistic hiring timeline if applicable
- Appropriate salary range for budget planning
- Defined supervision and authority structure

**Why This Matters:**
Case studies show dedicated staff is essential for success. Need to know if BRCC can actually staff this ministry appropriately.

### 5. Cultural Fit Assessment

**What We Need to Know:**
- BRCC's worship style and atmosphere
- Preaching approach and typical topics
- How controversial issues are handled
- Congregation's demographic makeup
- Digital presence strength
- Openness to change and adaptation

**Investigation Methods:**
- Honest self-assessment of BRCC's current culture
- Comparison to young adult preferences from research
- Review recent sermons for topics and style
- Assessment of website and social media presence
- Evaluation of leadership diversity (age, gender, ethnicity)

**Specific Cultural Questions:**
1. Worship style: Contemporary? Traditional? Blended?
2. Preaching: Expositional? Topical? Practical? Theological?
3. Topics: Are mental health, justice, doubt discussed openly?
4. Leadership: Are young adults, women, minorities represented?
5. Atmosphere: Formal? Casual? Welcoming?
6. Digital: Strong website and social media? Or weak?
7. Change capacity: Will congregation accept needed adaptations?

**Cultural Gaps to Assess:**
- Authenticity vs. production
- Room for questions and doubt
- Modern issue discussion (mental health, justice, sexuality)
- Leadership diversity and young representation
- Casual vs. formal atmosphere
- Digital-first engagement capacity

**Expected Outcome:**
- Honest assessment of BRCC's cultural alignment
- Identification of specific gaps needing attention
- Understanding of congregation's willingness to adapt
- Realistic appraisal of change capacity

**Why This Matters:**
"Moderate alignment with some gaps" is vague. Need specific understanding of what works, what needs adjustment, and what may be unchangeable.

**Investigation Methods:**

**Value Proposition Framework:**

### 8. Realistic Success Metrics Definition

**What We Need to Know:**
- What does Year 1/2/3 success look like for BRCC's size and context?
- What metrics matter beyond attendance?
- What indicates "on track" vs. "needs adjustment"?
- How patient will leadership actually be?

**Investigation Methods:**
- Research comparable church benchmarks (500-1,500 member churches)
- Define multi-year growth trajectory appropriate to BRCC
- Identify qualitative and quantitative metrics
- Discuss with leadership what they'd consider success

**Proposed Success Framework:**

**Year 1 Success Indicators:**
- **Attendance**: 20-40 regular participants (2x/month+)
- **Community**: Core group knows each other, genuine relationships forming
- **Structure**: 2-3 active small groups established
- **Engagement**: Positive feedback, people inviting friends
- **Foundation**: Systems and processes working
- **Leadership**: Young adult pastor hired, onboarded, building relationships

**Year 2 Success Indicators:**
- **Attendance**: 40-80 regular participants
- **Growth**: Small groups growing to 4-6 groups
- **Integration**: Young adults serving in broader church ministries
- **Spiritual fruit**: 2-3 baptisms, professions of faith, or significant testimonies
- **Momentum**: Word-of-mouth growth beginning
- **Stability**: Volunteer leadership emerging

**Year 3 Success Indicators:**
- **Attendance**: 60-100+ regular participants
- **Sustainability**: Volunteer leadership structure functioning
- **Church-wide impact**: Young adults in broader leadership roles
- **Transformation**: Multiple testimonies of life change
- **Organic growth**: Participants inviting others naturally
- **Culture**: Integrated into BRCC's identity

**Also Define Warning Signs:**
- Year 1: <15 participants after 6 months
- Year 2: No growth or declining attendance
- Any year: High turnover, conflict, burnout
- Disconnect between young adult ministry and broader church

**Expected Outcome:**
- Clear, realistic benchmarks for evaluation
- Agreement with leadership on what constitutes success
- Defined evaluation schedule (quarterly? annually?)
- Established triggers for "adjust strategy" vs. "persist"

**Why This Matters:**
Without clear metrics, success is subjective. Need alignment with leadership on realistic expectations before launch, not discovered during pressure moments.

## Research Update: Church Landscape & Market Share Findings (February 2026)

In February 2026, two additional research investigations were completed to address the competitive intelligence gaps identified above. The findings are documented in:
- `11-Church-Landscape-Analysis.md` — Survey of 30+ churches across the service area
- `12-Market-Share-Analysis.md` — Addressable population segmentation using Pew, Barna, Gallup, PRRI, and *The Great Dechurching* data

### Key Finding: The 25-32 Age Range Is Almost Entirely Unserved

Of the 30+ churches researched across New Palestine, McCordsville, Cumberland, Fortville, Greenfield, and east Indianapolis, **no church in BRCC's immediate service area offers dedicated programming for young adults ages 25-32**. The few churches with young adult ministry target younger ranges:

| Church | Location | YA Age Range | Threat Level |
|--------|----------|-------------|-------------|
| Realife Church | New Palestine/Greenfield | 18-24 | High |
| Brandywine Community | Greenfield | 18-30 (informal) | Moderate |
| Harvest Church | McCordsville | 18-early 30s | Low (small) |
| Northview Church | Indy/Fishers | 25+ | Moderate (distant) |

Large Indianapolis megachurches (ITOWN, Traders Point) draw young adults with production quality but lack dedicated young adult programming.

### Addressable Population: ~700-1,200 Young Adults

Of the ~5,000 young adults (ages 22-32) in BRCC's service area:

| Segment | Est. Count | Reachable by BRCC? | Strategic Priority |
|---------|-----------|---------------------|-------------------|
| Actively Churched | ~1,650 | Low (already have church home) | Not primary target |
| Nominally Churched | ~750 | Medium | Secondary target |
| De-Churched | ~900 | Medium-High (many open to return) | **Primary target** |
| Unchurched but Open | ~600 | High (willing if invited) | **Primary target** |
| Not Interested | ~1,150 | Low | Not primary target |

**Total realistically addressable: ~700-1,200 young adults** after applying willingness and response rate estimates to each segment.

### The De-Churched Opportunity

An estimated ~900 young adults in the area once attended church but stopped. Research from *The Great Dechurching* shows 51% are willing to return — they just want to be treated well and have genuine relationships. In Indiana, many grew up in church, moved to the area, and never reconnected. That's an estimated 360-500 people who are open to coming back with the right invitation.

Additional context on this segment:

- 75% of the de-churched left casually (drifted away, moved, got busy) — not because of anger or hurt
- The #1 reason mainstream evangelicals leave church is **moving to a new area** — exactly the dynamic happening in BRCC's service territory

### Strategic Takeaways

1. **Own the 25-32 age range.** No church in the immediate area serves this demographic with dedicated programming. This is a wide-open strategic position for BRCC — young professionals, young married couples, and new parents have no ministry home.

2. **Target McCordsville as the growth market.** Fastest-growing town in the area (6.62% annual growth), youngest median age (35.3), and no church there offers robust young adult programming. This is where the people are.

3. **The de-churched are the primary target.** Of the ~900 addressable young adults, the de-churched represent the largest reachable group (~360). Ministry messaging should specifically address why people left: judgment, shallowness, irrelevance, lack of genuine connection.

4. **Personal invitation is the #1 growth strategy.** Across all research sources, 63-67% of Americans say a personal invitation from a friend or family member would be effective in getting them to visit a church. Relational outreach outperforms marketing.

5. **Life transitions are the windows of opportunity.** Moving, marriage, and first child are peak openness moments. BRCC's location in a high-growth area means a constant stream of young adults experiencing these transitions.

6. **Compete on community, not production.** BRCC cannot outproduce ITOWN or Traders Point. But deep relationships, authentic community, real conversations, and consistent follow-up offer something megachurches cannot.

7. **Set realistic expectations.** Year 1: 20-50 regular participants. Year 3: 50-120. Year 5+: 70-150. These numbers represent meaningful capture of the addressable market and should not be seen as failure.

8. **The population is growing — time is an ally, but delay has a cost.** Hancock County's young adult population is expanding. A ministry planted now has a growing market ahead of it. However, other churches that establish young adult presence first will be harder to compete with later.

9. **Fill the young married couples gap.** Almost no church in the area serves this niche. It's a natural extension of BRCC's family-oriented strengths and the integrated 22-32 ministry approach.

10. **Build a strong digital presence.** Realife Church's 3,400+ Instagram followers and professional content set the local benchmark. Young adults discover churches through social media and web searches. BRCC must be findable.

## Conclusion: From Research to Strategy

The comprehensive young adult ministry research provides excellent external landscape analysis. The church landscape survey ([11 Church Landscape Analysis](11-Church-Landscape-Analysis.md)) and market share analysis ([12 Market Share Analysis](12-Market-Share-Analysis.md)) have now addressed the competitive intelligence gaps, confirming both the opportunity and its realistic scope.

**Key Principle:**
Strategy must be built on both external opportunity (demographic research) and internal reality (BRCC assessment). Neither alone is sufficient.

**Remaining Gaps — Internal BRCC Assessment:**
The external picture is now well-researched. The critical missing pieces are internal to BRCC:

1. **Current young adult count** — How many 22-32 year olds currently attend BRCC?
2. **Budget reality** — What funding is realistically available?
3. **Staffing options** — Existing staff capacity or need to hire?
4. **Leadership alignment** — Elder board and senior pastor support level?
5. **Cultural fit** — Worship style, openness to change, digital presence quality?

**Path Forward:**

1. **Gather internal BRCC data** — The Tier 1 investigations above remain essential
2. **Define realistic expectations with leadership** — Use the market share data to ground the conversation
3. **Develop BRCC's value proposition** — Now that the competitive landscape is mapped, articulate why a young adult would choose BRCC
4. **Make informed decision** — With both external opportunity and internal capacity understood

**The external research is now comprehensive. Completing the internal BRCC assessment transforms it into implementable strategy.**
